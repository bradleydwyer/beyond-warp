# Beyond — warp field study

A real-time WebGL 2 / Three.js interpretation of the side-on warp bubble in [this Star Trek Beyond clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gCCYWTAA2wQ).

The scene is generated in code: a rounded bow feeding several connected, uneven lobes, background stars that refract through the field, and a small procedural starship. Three appearances are available: luminous fluid, compressed environmental glow, and direct reprojections of the background into three bands. The reference footage is not used by the renderer.

## Run

```sh
git clone https://github.com/bradleydwyer/beyond-warp.git
cd beyond-warp
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite, normally **http://127.0.0.1:5173/**.

```sh
npm run build       # Static production files in dist/
npm run preview     # Preview the production build
npm test            # Browser / GPU smoke checks
```

Tests use installed Google Chrome, or Playwright Chromium if Chrome is unavailable. To install the latter, run `npx playwright install chromium`. A browser with WebGL 2 and hardware acceleration is required.

## Controls

- The page opens with the reference camera sequence playing automatically, with sound muted. Use the sound button to unmute during playback, or **Replay reference** to start again with audio. An explicit mute choice is kept for subsequent replays. Reduced-motion preferences, `?paused`, and explicit camera/time URLs skip autoplay.
- **Play reference** replays the original 4.96-second camera pullback with audio from the linked clip. It starts close to the bow, holds its initial framing, then pans and pulls back into the wide wake. The button resets the scene to the reference settings; press it again to replay.
- **Sound** beside that button mutes/unmutes the reference audio. **Space** pauses both camera and sound. Choosing another camera stops the sequence; leaving the tab pauses it. At the end, the camera settles into a wide view with space ahead of the bow, and the simulation keeps running. The audio plays once; use **Replay reference** to repeat the camera move and sound.
- **Tracking** matches the final wide frame: the nose at the left edge, a very small ship, upper lobes, and a broad lower wake.
- **Bow** matches the early close-up, showing the rounded compression front and refracted starlight ahead of the ship. You can open it directly with `?view=bow`.
- **Orbit** supports dragging, scrolling to zoom, and right-dragging to pan.
- **On board** looks forward from inside the bubble.
- **Flyby** starts with the ship on the right, then lets it overtake the tracking camera and drift left while the camera smoothly pulls back over eight seconds. The final view includes the entire field with room around both tips. The simulation keeps running after the camera settles; click **Flyby** again or press **R** to repeat. Settings are preserved, **Space** pauses the move, and another camera cancels it. Use `?view=flyby` to open this preset directly.
- **Field appearance** offers **Fluid glow** (the earlier luminous folds and gas), **Compressed glow** (bands illuminated by surrounding space), and **Compressed space** (the background reprojected along the upper edge, lower edge, and centre stream). Switching preserves the camera, simulation time, and settings; replay keeps the selected appearance. Compressed glow is selected initially; `?appearance=fluid` or `?appearance=space` opens either alternative.
- **Compressed space** reveals two extra controls: **Projection width** adjusts all three image bands from 50–400% (default 200%), and **Edge glow** adds brightness and a soft halo to the outer projections from 0–300% (default 0%). Edge glow uses the projected stars and nebula, leaving the centre stream and general background exposure unchanged. Both settings are retained when changing appearances or replaying a camera move.
- **Warp speed** changes star motion and fluid transport.
- **Turbulence** changes stirring, vorticity, and the thickness of the turbulent wake.
- **Field intensity** controls luminous fluid, concentrated glow, or reprojection visibility according to the selected appearance, independently of **Light bending**. In Compressed glow, **Bloom** also controls how far the glow spreads from the rim and centre stream.
- **Bloom** controls the soft glow, star halos, and horizontal flares from 0–200% (default 65%), including the anamorphic **Streak bloom**. At zero, stars keep their sharp motion streaks without the optical glow.
- **Star density** changes the number of streaking and distant stars from 0–200% (default 100%). At zero, only the nebula and field remain; that also removes the hero sources (see **Lens › Hero flares**). Bloom and star density choices are kept when replaying the reference.
- Turn **Field intensity** to zero to inspect only the background-star distortion. Compare **Light bending** at zero and 100% while paused.
- **Effects** (beside the quality menu) opens a collapsible panel of finer controls, grouped into Space, Field, Lens, Film and Experiments. Every one of them is on by default; setting a slider to 0% or unticking the box restores the earlier look, so each effect can be A/B compared while paused. All of them are kept when changing appearances or replaying a camera move. Each can also be set from the URL with the key shown in brackets below (for example `?crestThickness=1.5&energyRim=0`); values are clamped to the slider's range. Nebula density and Nebula highlights only modify the nebula, so they change nothing while Nebula brightness is 0%; Energy-conserving rim only modifies the interiors and crest, so it changes nothing while Interior darkness is 0%; Streak length and Streak tint only modify the streak, so they change nothing while Streak bloom is 0%. With every Film control at 0% / off, the film pass is skipped entirely, so the picture is exactly the one without it.
  - **Space › Nebula brightness** (`nebula`, 0–200%, default 100%) is the volumetric nebula behind the field: dark navy banks streaked along the flight path, densest below the field with a thinner, patchier bank above it, lit from above, with pale tops, darker bellies, filaments, dark dust lanes and long streaks. At 0% only the original faint fog remains.
  - **Space › Nebula density** (`nebulaDensity`, 0–200%, default 100%) changes how much of the sky the banks and filaments cover. At 0% it restores the same look as Nebula brightness at 0%.
  - **Space › Nebula highlights** (`nebulaGlow`, 0–200%, default 100%) adds the sparse pink-violet patches on the lit, dense gas and hot pink-white knots inside the banks. The gas in front of a knot scatters its light forward, so it glows through the bank. At 0% the banks stay blue.
  - **Space › Black level** (`blackLevel`, 0–200%, default 100%) lifts the darkest parts of the picture to a deep blue instead of black, including the dark bubble interiors. It is applied once to the final field image, never to the sky the field gathers its light from, so it cannot brighten the crest or the Compressed glow column. Nothing else adds this floor again, and **Lens › Vignette** darkens the corners only down to it. With Black level at 0%, the film finish's softness and speed-dust haze can still add about one level of light to the very darkest pixels of the close-up shots, as real haze would.
  - **Field › Interior darkness** (`interior`, 0–100%, default 92%) darkens the light seen through the bubble interiors, so they read as near-black ovals, darkest in the upper half. It scales with **Light bending**: at 0% bending the bubble no longer steers light away from its interior, so the dark ovals go too.
  - **Field › Interior shrink** (`interiorShrink`, 0–100%, default 60%) demagnifies the space seen through the interiors toward the field axis and softens it to match, so it reads as a dark, soft oval rather than patches of refracted gas. It also scales with Light bending.
  - **Field › Crest thickness** (`crestThickness`, 0–200%, default 100%) is the thick, lit crest just inside each edge, in all three appearances. It is a thin arc at the bow and thickens downstream, is brightest just inside the edge and fades inward, and is thinner and flatter on the lower edge. Its texture is chunky whitewater with dark gaps, carried by the fluid simulation, so it streams downstream with the flow. Its light is the surrounding space (reflected and, in Compressed glow, gathered into it), never emitted gas, so dark surroundings leave it dark.
  - **Field › Crest lighting (top/bottom)** (`crestLight`, 0–100%, default 85%) is a key light from above: how much dimmer the lower edges and downward-facing shell are than the top. The underside is never fully unlit, and in Compressed space the reprojected ribbons are only shaded gently. With this or Crest thickness on, the thin edge, the ribbons and the lens ring just inside the silhouette are never drawn darker than about half of the space behind them, so the lower edge never reads as a dark outline against bright gas.
  - **Field › Crest variation** (`crestVariation`, 0–150%, default 100%) breaks the crest into large bright and dim stretches, whitewater clumps with deep dark gaps and a churning skyline whose swells rise above the smooth silhouette on the lit top, all travelling downstream with the flow. It only ever dims the thin edge line, so bright stretches never flare into a flat bar.
  - **Lens › Streak bloom** (`streak`, 0–200%, default 100%) is an anamorphic streak: the brightest parts of the picture (bright stars, the ship's engines, the hottest crest highlights, hero flashes) throw long, thin horizontal streaks that turn blue as they reach out. It is taken from the HDR frame before the round bloom and scales with **Bloom**. It replaces the old faint five-tap horizontal flare, which cross-fades back in only below 25% (at 10%, for example, 60% of the old flare is added to 10% of the streak); at 0% the picture is exactly the earlier one.
  - **Lens › Streak length** (`streakLength`, 0–100%, default 75%) sets how far the streaks reach, from a short smear to the full width of the frame. Below 20% the streak fades out, and at 0% it is off.
  - **Lens › Streak tint** (`streakTint`, 0–100%, default 80%) is how blue the streaks turn as they reach out. The core of a streak stays close to the colour of its source; at 0% the whole streak keeps that colour.
  - **Lens › Hero flares** (`heroFlares`, 0–200%, default 100%) adds three bright, deterministic sources far behind the field, travelling with the stars. Where the field's edge passes over one, the edge around it lights up in a short caustic arc (in Compressed glow and Compressed space; Fluid glow has only the flash), and the source itself flashes, with a warm-edged round bloom and a streak, for about a tenth of a second. Both depend on the edge actually crossing the source: they are weighted by how steeply the source's path crosses the nearest edge, so a source gliding along a nearly level crest or lower edge stays a bright star rather than a glowing ball that rides the edge. In practice the flashes happen at the steep, rounded bow. The camera paths are unchanged: the first source is placed so the reference sequence meets it at the rounded bow at about 2 s, as in the clip (about 2.1 s in a 16:9 window), a little above the centre line so its streak runs beside the centre stream. The wide and bow views meet it at the bow tip about every 20 s. The other two are dimmer; they stay out of the reference shot and the default `?paused` still, and pass above the crests and below the lower edges of the wide view now and then as bright, streaking stars. At 0% they are gone.
  - **Lens › Star streaks** (`starStreaks`, 0–150%, default 100%) makes the brightest stars streak sideways instead of glowing as a round blob: part of each hot core is spread along a much longer, thinner line (a fixed angle, so it does not depend on speed), and the soft halo narrows and follows it. An anamorphic streak is horizontal on screen, so this fades out where the flight is not (looking down on the field in Orbit, or along it in On board); there the horizontal Streak bloom alone streaks the bright stars. Ordinary stars are unchanged.
  - **Lens › Vignette** (`vignette`, 0–200%, default 100%) deepens the darkening toward the corners of the picture, by up to about a third in the far corners; it starts well out from the centre, so the middle of the frame and the sky above the wide shot keep their light. It is the only vignette: the lens pass applies it in scene light (before tone mapping, like a real lens), on top of the gentle falloff the picture always had, so highlights in the corners keep their sparkle. It darkens the corners down to the deep-blue floor that **Black level** lifts black to, never below it, so the corners stay deep blue and Black level stays the one control for the darkest pixels. With the letterbox on, it follows the 2.39:1 picture, not the whole screen. At 0% only the original falloff remains.
  - **Experiments › Energy-conserving rim** (`energyRim`, default on) couples the interiors to the crest: the light a bubble's interior loses is gathered into its crest, so the big downstream bubbles have the brightest crests, and a bright crest stretch casts a soft shadow into the interior beneath it. Seen face-on (from above in Orbit) the crest is no longer a grazing rim, so the boost falls away. Off, the interior darkening is uniform and every crest has the same gain.
  - **Film › Grain** (`grain`, 0–200%, default 100%) is fine, soft film grain: two layers of smoothed value noise a little over a pixel across, turned off the pixel grid. It is multiplicative and strongest in the mid-tones, so it never lifts the blacks, plus a sub-level dither that breaks banding in the deep shadows (and fades out at pure black). It is seeded by the simulation frame, never the wall clock: it changes once per simulation frame during playback and a paused frame stays bit-identical.
  - **Film › Softness** (`softness`, 0–200%, default 100%) is a slight overall softness, like the photographed clip: a fine blend with a 4-tap blur about a pixel wide. It also takes the edge off the 1-pixel bow lines.
  - **Film › Depth of field** (`focus`, 0–200%, default 100%) defocuses the space beyond the field when the camera is close to it. Each pixel's view ray is compared with the field's own profile: rays that pass near the field stay sharp (the bow, the crest, the ship), rays that pass far from it see distant space and are softened. The strength follows how close the camera is to the field, so it is strong in the bow close-up and the opening of the reference sequence and almost gone in the wide shots. At the default it stays within the same fine blur as Softness, so the thin star dashes ahead of the bow stay distinct, as in the clip; above 100% it mixes in a wider, half-resolution blur that is horizontal only, so defocused stars become longer, softer dashes rather than faint round smudges.
  - **Film › Speed dust** (`dust`, 0–200%, default 100%) is large, soft, out-of-focus gas close to the lens, smeared along the flight and whipping past at three depths (the nearest has the fewest, largest and brightest clumps), plus the faint haze it lays over the frame, which also greys the colours behind it a little. Raising it adds more and brighter clumps rather than a grey cast. It is lit only by the space around it (near and across the frame), so it brightens over the gas banks, stays faint over the dark interiors and disappears if space is black. It moves only with the travelled distance, along the on-screen flight direction, so it pauses with the simulation and fades when the camera looks along the field. Like Depth of field, it is strongest close to the field (the opening of the reference sequence) and almost gone in the wide shots.
  - **Film › Grade** (`grade`, 0–200%, default 100%) is a blue-teal grade: red is pulled out of the blues toward cyan and a little blue moves into green, while the same red axis pushes the existing pink-violet gas toward magenta, so magenta appears only occasionally. It only shifts hue: each pixel keeps its brightness, so the grade neither darkens the blue sky nor lifts anything. Close to the field (the opening of the reference sequence and the bow view), where everything is seen through the near gas, it is also greyer, like the clip's hazy opening; the wide shots keep their saturated blue and only the most saturated colour is tamed, a little. The deep shadows keep Black level's blue. There is no extra highlight shoulder, because the filmic tone mapping already rolls the highlights off, so a hero flash can still reach white. Every step keeps black at black, so the deep-blue lift of **Black level** is applied once only.
  - **Film › Letterbox (sequences)** (`letterbox`, default on) adds 2.39:1 bars during the reference and Flyby camera moves, in the page's background colour, when the window's width is at least 1.2 times its height but less than 2.39 times (on a 16:9 screen, for example). The 1280 × 536 framing of the clip is already 2.39:1 and needs no bars, and portrait phones and near-square windows are left full-frame, where the bars would leave only a thin strip.
  - **Bypass all effects (A/B)** shows the original look in one click without moving any slider (`?effectsBypass=1`). **Defaults** restores the tuned values; **All off** sets every effect to 0% / off.
- **Space** pauses; **R** restarts; **H** hides the controls; **F** toggles fullscreen.
- **Save frame** downloads the rendered scene as a PNG without UI.
- Reduced-motion preferences start the scene paused.

## Rendering

`src/fluid.js` implements a GPU fluid solver on a 512 × 256 grid of the shell's axial and angular coordinates. It uses midpoint semi-Lagrangian advection, clamped MacCormack correction for density, counter-rotating forces, vorticity confinement, and a 12-iteration pressure projection. A downstream velocity constraint after forcing and projection prevents ripples from reversing toward the bow while allowing angular rolling. The simulation advances at a fixed 60 Hz. The angular boundary wraps around the shell; the axial boundary has an inlet and outlet. This follows the stable-fluids approach described in [NVIDIA GPU Gems, chapter 38](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-vi-beyond-triangles/chapter-38-fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-gpu).

`src/field.js` fits separate upper and lower envelopes to the final decoded reference frame (1280 × 536, approximately 4.92 seconds). Upper crests are near 28%, 51%, and 85% of the frame width. The lower wake is broader and smoother, and the internal fluid band remains near mid-height. The wide camera spans about 260 world units while the procedural ship is about 1.6 units long. The opening close-up spans 24 units. An analytic paraboloid rounds the first ten units of the bow, preserving its curvature when viewed close up.

The envelope is uploaded as two floating-point lookup textures containing heights and derivatives. Shape-preserving cubic interpolation avoids overshoot. Passing environmental density changes each lobe's dimensions and spacing, with downstream sizes kept ordered. Upper and lower contours have different amounts of variation, keeping the total envelope broad through the joins. The rear half of the final lobe is an ellipsoidal cap: both edges meet the centre stream at a shared, rounded tip. The field fades along the last lobe, with the fade interval following its changing dimensions.

In Fluid glow, `src/shaders.js` integrates the shell along adaptively spaced camera-ray samples. Surface displacement, ribbons, and folds come from transported fluid dye. A separate, thin 3D shear layer creates the rolling centre band; its folds and branching threads use that same moving fluid, rather than a fixed line.

Compressed space retains the curved-ray distortion and reprojects the background three times, across the upper edge, lower edge, and centre stream. Each ribbon squeezes a full-height background column into its adjustable width, preserving horizontal alignment so the same stars and nebula features appear in all three copies. Eight filtered samples split the compression footprint, preserving horizontal streak detail without flickering or blurring both dimensions. A modest compression-dependent gain keeps the minified image visible. Optional Edge glow brightens and spreads the light from the two outer images; it does not gather ambient light or add luminous gas. This is an artistic screen-space optical mapping, not a physically traced mirror reflection.

Compressed glow adds concentrated light to those reprojections. A live 64-pixel cube probe captures the same moving stars and nebula in all six directions, allowing off-camera space to illuminate the bands. Broad background samples retain variation along the rim and centre stream. Their glow inherits the incoming light's colour and brightness; black surroundings produce no glow. A soft luminance shoulder limits hot highlights, while Field intensity controls the concentrated light and Bloom controls its halo. The probe is fixed near the bow and approximates illumination across the full field; it is only updated when space changes, and only needed in Compressed glow.

The nebula is a world-space 3D density and colour field. Accumulated ship travel moves it past the ship and through the warp region. The same seeded cloud field drives envelope pressure and fluid stirring. In Fluid glow, it also drives surface luminosity and colour, with extra illumination near the shell and a broader plume below the field. It is not a screen-attached background, and brightness changes follow the clouds moving through the scene.

Large cloud regions vary the coverage and blue, teal, and violet colours. Their shapes distort smaller scales into wisps and dark dust lanes, giving the passing nebula distinct dense and sparse patches without random per-frame flicker.

The backdrop adds a brighter volumetric nebula on top of that fog (**Nebula brightness**). Six slices behind the field are composited back to front with emission and absorption, so nearer slices pass faster and the gas has depth. Each slice is two-level domain-warped fbm built from Hermite-smoothed lookups of the same seeded lattice: a body that forms banks, ridged noise for bright filaments, a second ridge for dark dust lanes, and fine streaks that erode the banks into wisps. Everything finer than the banks themselves is drawn out two to four times along the flight path, and a long streak field splits the banks into bright sheets and dark gaps, so the gas reads as streaming past at warp rather than as billowing cumulus. A key light from above follows the density gradient: where the gas thins upward a bank has a pale lit top, where it thickens upward it is the shadowed belly of the bank above. Sparse hot cores emit pink-white light, and each slice carries the light of the cores behind it, forward-scattered by the gas in front (**Nebula highlights**). A shared, bounded, billowing upper boundary keeps the dense banks below the field; above it is a thinner, patchier bank at about half the density. The body of the gas is a dark navy and the lit gas much brighter, bright dense gas desaturates toward white, and a gentle luminance shoulder stops overlapping banks bleaching while leaving the hot cores well above the body. The same backdrop feeds the light probe and the extended scene capture, so Compressed space and Compressed glow reproject and gather a sky that finally has content. The concentrated rim and centre light are exposed for the captured scene's average brightness, keeping them from saturating into a uniform white line over bright gas while dark surroundings keep the full gain. Where the gathered light is still far brighter than that average (looking down into a bank from above), a soft knee on its own luminance caps the column, the edge and the crest before they glare to white. These nebula-only adjustments follow both Nebula brightness and Nebula density, so either at 0% restores the earlier look. The black-level lift is added once, to the final field image, after all of this: the sky the field gathers from and the light probe never contain it.

The thick crest, the dark interiors, and the crest's bright and dim stretches are shared by all three appearances. For each pixel, the straight view ray is solved against the local elliptical cross-section of the field. That gives how deep the ray passes inside the shell, where it enters the front face, and which silhouette it is nearest; a marched minimum distance would band deep inside the field. A ray running along the field (seen from ahead, behind or at a slant in Orbit) reaches its deepest point far from its closest approach to the axis, so the interior darkness also samples ten points along the ray's path through the field and keeps the deepest; the maximum of smooth terms stays smooth, and rays crossing the field are unchanged. Interior darkness continues almost to the end of the tail, so the rounded tail cap still closes over a dark interior.

The crest is a lit shell band. Its brightness profile, box-filtered over the pixel and antialiased at its outer edge, is the optical path through a thin shell (whose relative thickness grows downstream, and is about half as thick on the lower, unlit side) at the ray's normalised distance from the axis, times a grazing-angle Fresnel term and an inward window: brightest just inside the silhouette, fading inward. The band reflects the space above it (and, in Compressed glow, the gathered surroundings; in Compressed space and Fluid glow, the average sky around the camera) off the dye-rippled front face. After a luminance shoulder, chunky whitewater clumps (a lattice lookup addressed only by transported dye), the simulation's own foam, and the broad patches modulate it, so the texture is not flattened; a final, gentler shoulder and a capped whitening keep its brightest clumps from glaring. A crest only a few pixels deep reads the box-filtered dye instead of the full-resolution fluid, which would bead along its edge. The churning skyline is read from the box-filtered dye at the silhouette point, so it is continuous across the silhouette and its swells can rise above it without leaving specks. In Compressed space the crest is about half as bright and keeps the colour of the space it reflects, so the reprojected ribbons stay the mode's signature. In Fluid glow the same band is added after the march, seen through the luminous gas, alongside the whitewater layer integrated under the luminous surface. Where the nebula is bright, the gas Fluid glow integrates in front of the field occludes the backdrop instead of adding to it twice, and the concentrated edge light in Compressed glow gets a softer shoulder so a bright bank in its column does not bloom into a lens-like hot spot.

The interiors take the light that passes through them, pull it toward the field axis, blur it to match the demagnification, and dim it; the black-level lift then keeps the darkest of them deep blue.

The fluid simulation carries a fourth, whitewater channel. Foam is spawned where the transported dye has sharp fronts and the flow shears, carried downstream with the dye (including the MacCormack correction), and decays over about a second. A 64 × 32, box-filtered copy of the dye, rebuilt after each step, supplies the crest's large bright and dim stretches, its churning skyline, the clumps along the thin edge, and the energy-conserving coupling without the aliasing of sparse taps. Everything is driven by the fixed-step simulation and the travelled distance, so paused frames stay identical.

Stars and nebula share the faster lateral transport seen in the opening shot. Each star's streak length follows its actual velocity and a 28 ms exposure. Filtered HDR cores, soft halos, and the optical bloom produce bright passing streaks while keeping subpixel highlights stable. The larger star volume recycles stars outside the visible frame. With **Star streaks** on, the sparsest, brightest stars (about the top few percent) also reach out a fixed angle further, and their core is partly moved onto a thin line along that length, so the round HDR bloom sees a long line rather than a hot point. Star streaks switch a shader define, so at 0% the original star shaders run unchanged.

The lens is anamorphic. A streak pass box-filters the HDR frame (after the field, ship and near stars, before the round bloom) down to a quarter of its width and half its height through a soft-knee threshold with a gentle shoulder, so a big flash does not throw a far heavier streak than a bright star. It then halves the width again and again with Gaussian-weighted horizontal-only filters (down to about 12 texels across) and climbs back up, blending each finer level with the blurred coarser one. That blend sets the streak length; a per-level tint that compounds on the way up keeps a streak's core near the source's colour and turns its far reaches blue. The lens pass adds the result before the vignette and tone mapping. The vignette lives only in the lens pass, in scene light: the original gentle falloff, deepened by **Vignette**. All of it is deterministic and costs well under a millisecond.

Hero sources are three instanced point sprites, a fixed angle in size, that move with the travelled distance like the stars (and are split into background and foreground in the same way). Each frame the CPU projects them, and the field's upper and lower silhouette, to the screen. The volume shader for Compressed glow and Compressed space adds a caustic around each source that lies behind the field: a thin arc and a hotter focus, confined to a few pixels either side of the silhouette, so the edge lights up only where it passes near the source. A small pass before the streak and the round bloom adds the source's flash, which follows the source's distance to the projected silhouette; both lens stages then spread it like any other highlight.

Stars occupy both sides of the field, so reverse orbit views also show passing space. Streaks between the camera and the field's bounds are rendered in a separate foreground pass, after the optical distortion. These near stars stay straight instead of being treated as background light.

The shader also integrates a curved viewing ray through a smooth refractive-index field around the same lobes. It updates the ray direction from the component of `grad(log n)` perpendicular to the ray, and uses the outgoing direction to sample the star background. Smaller surface ripples contribute more gently to the lens than to the visible folds. Lensing works independently of emission, including when field intensity is zero. The ship is composited separately and stays stable.

The general idea of tracing curved rays and filtering the distorted star field is inspired by real-time black-hole renderers, including [Eric Bruneton's beam-tracing renderer](https://ebruneton.github.io/black_hole_shader/). This implementation uses a graded optical index around a fictional warp field; it does not use that renderer's Schwarzschild model or precomputed geodesic tables.

`src/main.js` manages the scene, cameras, render targets, HDR bloom, film optics, tone mapping, antialiasing, and controls. The background target uses multisampling, mipmaps, and a view 1.8 times wider than the visible frame. A second, camera-centred cube capture supplies light beyond that extended view. Rays retain their bending at the screen boundary; there is no viewport-edge distortion fade or offset clamp. The lower-resolution cube fallback is blended only at the outer edge of the extended capture. Full-screen passes explicitly disable depth testing/writing to prevent alternating-frame feedback from reused depth buffers.

The film finish (`src/film.js`) is the last pass, after tone mapping and FXAA, so the antialiasing cannot smear its grain; it works on the display-referred image, where a grade behaves like a colourist's. A three-pass half-resolution Gaussian of the frame (two passes across, one down) supplies the depth of field and the light on the speed dust, and a four-tap kernel the overall softness. The dust and the depth-of-field mask need only the camera, the field profile texture and the travelled distance, so no depth buffer is involved. The pass then grades, adds grain and mattes the letterbox. It does not vignette (the lens pass does, once) and it does not lift the blacks (Black level does, once, in the field shaders); its grade keeps black at black and adds no second highlight shoulder on top of the tone mapping. It costs about a tenth of a millisecond at 1280 × 536.

`src/ship.js` builds the original stand-in model from geometry, including a saucer, nacelles, hull, windows, and emissive engine strips.

This is an artistic real-time approximation: the fluid solver is 2D flow wrapped through a 3D shell, and curved rays sample an extended background view with a lower-resolution, all-direction cube fallback. It is not a relativistic spacetime solver or a full 3D CFD reconstruction of the movie effect.

## Validation

The browser checks exercise actual GPU rendering, all three field appearances, switching modes without resetting, stars in reverse orbit views, shader compilation, identical paused frames, evolving and sustained fluid flow, variable but ordered lobe sizes, background refraction with the glow disabled, the four static cameras and Flyby preset, quality settings, ship visibility, PNG export, mobile layout, and reduced motion. Controlled black, red, and blue off-camera environments verify the optical glow's source, colour, and controls. The Effects check confirms that every effect control changes the frame, that restoring them restores the frame exactly, that Bypass shows the all-off frame without moving a slider, that Defaults and All off work in one click, that the controls survive appearance switches and replays, and that URL parameters set them. It also checks that Compressed space never draws a pure-black outline, and that the opened panel fits, with every control reachable, at 390 × 844, 375 × 667, 844 × 390 and 1280 × 536. A film check at 16:9 confirms that the letterbox appears only during the camera sequences and goes away with its toggle, that the grain holds still while paused, and that the film pass does not lift the darkest pixels. The lens check takes the Bow camera to the moment the first hero source crosses the rounded front: the nose must flash (and not glare to white), the flash must be bit-identical between paused frames and restore exactly after Hero flares is toggled, and the streak must brighten the flash's row well to its left. A directional sky checks that refraction continues at the viewport boundary; profile checks verify that varying lobes retain a closed rear cap. Flyby checks cover smooth motion, the camera falling behind the ship, full-field framing, pause/resume, and continued simulation after the move. Screenshots are written to the ignored `artifacts/` directory.

For repeatable inspection, `?paused` starts paused and `window.warp` exposes `pause()`, `play()`, `seek(seconds)`, `advance(seconds)`, `setView(name)`, `render()`, and a read-only `fieldShape` snapshot. `seek()` reconstructs up to ten seconds of simulation history; `advance()` runs up to sixty seconds of additional fixed simulation steps.

`src/reference-sequence.js` fits global Bezier curves to camera landmarks measured from the clip. These smooth acceleration through the pullback and settle the camera to a stop in the final framing. During audible playback, the audio clock drives the camera and world travel, so dropped rendering frames cannot accumulate audiovisual drift. Automatic playback starts muted on the animation clock; unmuting seeks the audio to the current position and switches clocks without restarting. After the audio ends, the simulation continues on the animation clock with the camera held wide. If audio cannot load or play, the camera sequence continues silently.

Downloaded reference video/frames and development screenshots are ignored by Git and are not included in the build. The short reference audio track is included separately in `public/audio/`.
